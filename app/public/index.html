Whole-frame transition and training

Each input includes the ball and the 20 outfield players, with their 1 m cells, signed one-second velocities, and distances to the ball. Players are ordered by depth within each team from its own goal, and keep their identity across the transition.

One regularized regression maps the entire snapshot to 42 mean displacement coordinates. A single correlated Gaussian draw gives a displacement vector for the whole frame. Rounding yields integer cell movements, which determine both the next positions and velocities. The backward tool sums the selected entity’s marginal cell probabilities in the target area.

Leave-one-match-out validation on nine games selected the regression penalty and covariance shrinkage. Training required the same 20 outfield players and ball at the previous, current, and next snapshots. Substitutions, red cards, and missing tracks can leave a frame outside the model.

The fitted covariance captures co-movement around the predicted positions. The model uses a single Gaussian per snapshot and can place players off the pitch. It has no explicit rule for passes, collisions, or possession.

One-second transition equation

For each tracked outfield player and the ball, Sₜ = (Cₜ, Vₜ). The position Cₜ is the 1 m cell and Vₜ = Cₜ − Cₜ₋₁ is signed displacement over the previous second. This counts net displacement on each axis.

The fitted model assigns a probability to each next displacement d. Then Sₜ₊₁ = (Cₜ + d, d). The discrete forward equation is pₜ₊₁(s′) = Σₛ pₜ(s) P(s, s′). The viewer starts with all mass at the observed state. The backward region probability sums the next-cell probabilities whose centers lie inside the selected area.

Training counts next displacements by velocity bin, with an optional pitch-zone group. Sparse groups borrow probability from pooled counts. The model forecasts each entity independently.

Spatial state and equations

Each tracked outfield player and the ball have a state (x, y, vx, vy). The model estimates velocity from earlier tracking in the same clip and uses zero when that history is missing. Players and the ball have separate parameters.

Position drifts with velocity, and velocity decays toward zero. Independent Gaussian position and velocity noise spread the forecast. The generator for each entity is L f = v·∇ₓf − λv·∇ᵥf + (Dₓ/2)Δₓf + (Dᵥ/2)Δᵥf. The full generator is the sum over entities.

Forward. Solve ∂ₜp = L* p from the observed state. The app draws the position marginal of its Gaussian solution for each entity.

Backward. Solve ∂ₜu = −Lu with u(T) = 1 inside the selected region and 0 outside. The displayed result is the probability of being there at the horizon.

Forecasts use fixed player identities and independent motion noise. Team coordination, collisions, possession and pitch boundaries are outside this model. The pitch clips the display while the forecast can assign probability outside it.

    Equations and state definitions

    Forward. Start with probability 1 at the state of the selected frame. The row distribution evolves as dp/dt = pQ, so p(t+h) = p(t) exp(hQ).

    Backward. Select a target at terminal time T. The payoff g is 1 in that target state and 0 elsewhere. The probability from each starting state satisfies ∂u/∂t = −Qu, with u(T) = g and u(t) = exp((T−t)Q)g. This is the chance of being in the target at the selected horizon.

    Each state encodes the team in possession and the ball’s third from that team’s attacking direction. Frames without a recorded team use a seventh state that includes loose balls and stoppages. This finite-state model tracks those categories through time. Player coordinates, formations, and scores are outside its state space.

    Each off-diagonal entry of Q is a transition rate per second. The diagonal is the negative sum of the outward rates. A continuous-time generator allows forecasts between observation times and at different horizons.
